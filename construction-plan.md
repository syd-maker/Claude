# Building Two Elevated Homes

The existing structures aren't livable, so both units are new elevated construction. This is
now the largest number in the plan — and the binding constraint.

> **Planning estimates, not quotes.** Keys construction pricing is not well published. The
> single most valuable next step is three real bids (§6).

---

## 1. The headline

| | Stick-built | **Modular** | Saved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Residence, 1,200 sf | $1,018,000 | **$742,500** | $275,500 |
| Rental, 1,500 sf (3BR) | $1,238,125 | **$883,125** | $355,000 |
| **Both** | **$2,179,875** | **$1,511,875** | **$668,000** |

**Modular is the single biggest lever in this entire project — roughly 28%, or $668,000.**

Modular homes rated to **180 mph** are available for the Keys and set on pilings or stilts.
Turnkey Keys modular has been quoted at **under $200/sq ft for the structure**, against
$350–800/sq ft for Florida coastal stick-built. The factory does the work where labor isn't
scarce, then it's craned onto your columns.

**Get modular quotes before you get anything else priced.**

## 2. Build the rental at 3 bedrooms, not 2

Rental revenue scales **faster** than construction cost.

| Beds | sq ft | Build | VR revenue | Net/yr | Yield |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1,200 | $742,500 | $58,657 | $41,646 | 5.6% |
| **3** | **1,500** | **$883,125** | **$74,449** | **$52,859** | **6.0%** |
| 4 | 1,800 | $1,023,750 | $90,241 | $64,071 | 6.3% |

**2BR → 3BR: +$140,625 build, +$15,792/yr revenue, 12.5-year payback.** Since you only get
*one* rental unit, making it bigger is the main lever you have. Build the 3BR.

*Confirm first:* required parking, and any occupancy cap on Marathon's vacation rental license.
Those can bind before square footage does.

## 5. What closes the gap, ranked

**1. Modular instead of stick-built — saves $355,000 on Phase A.** Biggest single lever, costs
you nothing but a supplier choice. Do this first.

**2. Build smaller.** A 1,200 sf 3BR instead of 1,500 sf saves roughly $140,000. Tight, but
Keys rentals sell on outdoor living and location, not square footage — and you have a garden.

**3. Phase harder than Phase A.** Garden, kiosk, and painting-under-cover need **no buildings
at all.** Open the business first for roughly $350,000, run it while living off-site, then build
the home once there's revenue and a track record to refinance against. Slower and less
comfortable, but it drops the up-front number by more than anything else on this list.

**4. Negotiate the purchase price down — your rebuild cost is the argument.** The property
requires $1.5M of construction before anyone can live there. That is precisely why no
conventional buyer can touch it, and it should be reflected in the price. Bring these numbers
to the seller; they're not a bluff, they're the reason her buyer pool is small.

**5. Equity partner for the remainder.** After 1–4, whatever gap is left is an equity
conversation, not a debt one. You bring the deal, the vision, the marketing, and the operating
business; they bring capital for a share.

---

## 6. The next actual step

**Get three real bids before refining any of these numbers:**

1. A **Keys modular supplier** with 180 mph rating and piling experience — ask for turnkey,
   set on your columns, including transport and crane
2. A **Keys general contractor** for stick-built comparison
3. A **hybrid** — modular box, locally built foundation and site work

Also confirm early, because each can change the design:
- FEMA base flood elevation (sets column height, which drives cost)
- Required parking count
- Sewer connection and impact fees — Marathon's can be substantial
- Whether the covered ground level under an elevated home counts against floor area

**These estimates will move once real bids land. Treat every number above as a planning
placeholder that exists to size the problem, not to budget against.**
