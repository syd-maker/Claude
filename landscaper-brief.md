# Landscaper Brief — Request for Bid

Send this to every landscaper you approach. Identical scope to each one means the bids are
actually comparable, which is the only way to negotiate well.

---

## The project

A **native butterfly and bird garden** on ~1.5 lots on 74th Street, Marathon, forming the
centerpiece of a painting-experience business, garden admission, and coffee stand. The garden
is not decoration — **it is the product.** Guests pay to sit in it and paint the butterflies
and birds it attracts.

The site currently has significant debris, invasive vegetation, and a long-running backyard
chicken operation.

---

## ⚠️ Non-negotiable condition — read before bidding

> ## **NO PESTICIDES OR INSECTICIDES. EVER. ANYWHERE ON SITE.**
>
> This includes systemic insecticides, **neonicotinoid-treated nursery stock**, "preventive"
> treatments, and anything applied to turf or ornamentals.
>
> **The butterflies are the business.** A single broad-spectrum application destroys the entire
> product and years of establishment. Any bidder who cannot commit to this in writing, and
> guarantee that plant material arrives untreated, should not bid.
>
> Herbicides may be used **only** for initial invasive removal, by prior written approval,
> spot-applied, before any butterfly planting goes in.

**This clause goes in the contract, not just the conversation.** Most landscape crews spray by
default. Assume yours will unless the contract forbids it and the crew is briefed directly.

---

## Scope of work — please bid each phase separately

Separate line items let us stage the work as funding allows and compare bidders fairly.

### Phase 1 — Clearing and remediation
- Remove debris, derelict materials, and accumulated waste
- Remove and properly dispose of invasive vegetation (Brazilian pepper, Australian pine,
  lead tree, etc.) — identify what you find
- Remediate soil in the former chicken area: **testing before planting is required** —
  pathogens, heavy metals, nitrogen loading. Guests including children will sit in this garden
- Preserve and protect all existing native trees. **Survey and flag before any clearing.**
  Mature natives are worth years we cannot buy back
- Grading and drainage

### Phase 2 — Infrastructure
- Irrigation, zoned separately for establishment vs. mature planting
- Path substrate and edging
- Windbreak hedge on the windward side (butterflies will not fly in strong wind — this is
  functional, not aesthetic)
- Soil amendment and imported planting medium

### Phase 3 — Hardscape
- Primary ADA-accessible path from parking through the commercial areas — **required by law**
- Secondary garden paths, surface to be specified
- Boardwalk / decking where grade or drainage requires
- Seating nodes and pavilion base preparation

### Phase 4 — Planting
See plant list below. Bid by size (1 gal / 3 gal / 7 gal / field-grown) and quantity.

### Phase 5 — Establishment and ongoing maintenance
- 12-month establishment care, replacement warranty
- Ongoing maintenance — please quote **monthly**, seasonally adjusted
- **Explicit confirmation of the no-pesticide protocol in maintenance**

## Plant list — South Florida / Keys natives only

**No non-native invasives.** Specifically: use native **Lantana involucrata**, never
*Lantana camara*.

### Larval host plants (where butterflies lay eggs)
| Plant | Supports |
|---|---|
| **Corkystem passionflower**, passionvine | **Zebra Longwing — the Florida state butterfly**, Gulf Fritillary |
| Wild lime, torchwood | Giant Swallowtail, Schaus' Swallowtail |
| Cassia / senna | Sulphurs |
| Milkweed (native spp.) | Monarch, Queen |
| Pipevine | Polydamas Swallowtail |

### Nectar plants (where adults feed — these carry the show)
Firebush (*Hamelia patens*) · blue porterweed · **native** lantana (*L. involucrata*) ·
blanketflower · Spanish needle · necklacepod · Jamaica caper · wild coffee · buttonsage

### Canopy, structure, and bird food
Gumbo limbo · seagrape · pigeon plum · silver buttonwood · Jamaican dogwood · thatch palm ·
cocoplum · beautyberry

### Design intent to price around
**Shaded, dense jungle corridors opening into sunny, nectar-packed clearings.** Butterflies
need direct sun; a fully canopied garden will have none. The clearings need maximum sun and
wind protection.

## Questions for every bidder

1. Have you built a pollinator or butterfly garden before? Where can I see it?
2. Can you commit **in writing** to the no-pesticide protocol, including sourcing untreated stock?
3. Which Keys native nurseries do you source from?
4. Who physically does the work, and how will you brief them on the spray prohibition?
5. What's your replacement warranty on plant material?
6. What does establishment look like — how long until this reads as mature?
7. What would you do differently from what I've described?

## Separately: contact Florida Keys Mosquito Control

**Before planting anything.** The district sprays for mosquitoes across the Keys, and
adulticides kill butterflies — this is documented, and no-spray zones already exist elsewhere in
the Keys specifically to protect imperiled butterflies.

**Call 305-292-7190** or email questions@keysmosquito.org and ask what designation or
accommodation is available for a butterfly habitat property.

If the answer is that your property cannot be excluded from spraying, **that materially changes
this business** and you need to know before you spend six figures on habitat. Make this call
early.
