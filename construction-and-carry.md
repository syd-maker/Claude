# Debt Coverage, Construction Timeline, and the Carry Gap

Answers to two questions: how much of the mortgage the business covers, and how long the
build takes — including the part that gets missed, which is what you pay while it happens.

---

## 1. How much of the mortgage can the business pay?

**Good news, and it's better than expected.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Mortgage payment ($800k @ 6.75%) | $5,189/mo · $62,265/yr |
| NOI (seasonal model, $65 ticket) | $99,541/yr |
| **DSCR (coverage ratio)** | **1.60** |
| **Business covers** | **160% of the mortgage** |
| Annual surplus | $37,276 |

Lenders typically want a DSCR of 1.20–1.25. **You'd clear it comfortably.**

| Loan size | DSCR | Annual surplus |
|---|---:|---:|
| $600,000 | 2.13 | $52,842 |
| $800,000 | 1.60 | $37,276 |
| $1,000,000 | 1.28 | $21,709 |
| $1,200,000 | **1.07** | $6,143 |

**$1.2M is your ceiling** — at DSCR 1.07 there's no margin for a bad season.

### This opens a financing door
A **DSCR loan** qualifies on the *property's* income rather than your personal income. It's a
real product for income-producing property, and it's the natural answer to the
self-employment problem in `financing-reality.md`.

The catch: it requires the property to already be producing income. So it isn't your
acquisition loan — **it's your refinance.** Build with construction or seller financing, then
refinance into a DSCR loan once the business is operating and can document its own numbers.
That's a genuine, achievable path, and it's worth designing the whole plan around.

### Paying it down
| | Standard payments | Surplus applied to principal |
|---|---:|---:|
| Year 5 | 6.1% paid off | **33.7%** |
| Year 10 | 14.7% paid off | **81.0%** |
| Paid off in | 30 years | **11.7 years** |

Putting the $37,276 surplus straight at principal retires an $800k mortgage in
**under 12 years.**

## 3. The carry gap — the real problem

During that entire period there is **no revenue, and nowhere for you to live.** The RV/trailer
option is closed (Marathon doesn't permit it), so you're paying rent somewhere else *and* a
mortgage on a construction site.

| Monthly during construction | |
|---|---:|
| Mortgage | $5,189 |
| Property tax | $1,000 |
| Builder's risk / vacant property insurance | $1,600 |
| Utilities, security, misc | $400 |
| **Your rent elsewhere** | **$2,750** |
| **TOTAL MONTHLY BURN** | **$10,939** |

> **23 months (best case): $251,592**
> **42 months (worst case): $459,429**
>
> Pure negative carry. Zero revenue. This is the single largest hole in the plan.

## 4. The fix: build the duplex first

Don't build all three units at once. **Sequence them so the property starts paying for itself
and housing you as early as possible.**

| Phase | Months | What happens | Burn | Revenue | Net |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0–11 | Permits, demo, remediation, **garden planted** | $10,939/mo | $0 | −$120,329 |
| 2 | 11–22 | **Coffee kiosk + garden admission + painting under cover** | $10,939/mo | $13,224/mo | **+$25,131** |
| 3 | 22–34 | **Duplex done — live in one, rent the other** | $8,189/mo | $17,078/mo | **+$106,668** |

| | All-at-once | Phased |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total carry | $459,438 | **$95,198** |
| First revenue | Month 42 | **Month 11** |
| Stop paying outside rent | Month 42 | **Month 22** |

> **Saves roughly $364,000 of negative carry.**

### Why this works
- **The garden needs the lead time anyway.** Planting in phase 1 costs nothing extra and the
  garden is mature when guests arrive.
- **The coffee kiosk and garden don't need buildings.** A kiosk permits fast; painting can run
  under a covered structure or tent long before any building is finished.
- **The duplex houses you.** Live in one half, rent the other. That kills your outside rent
  *and* starts rental income roughly 18 months earlier.
- **Your permanent residence gets built last, from operating cash flow rather than borrowed
  money.** It's the piece that generates no revenue, so it should be funded by profit, not debt.

### The honest caveat
Phase 2 revenue assumes the garden and guest areas are **physically separated from the
construction zone** — noise, dust, and safety would otherwise wreck the experience you're
selling. On 1.5 lots this is achievable, but it has to be designed in from the site plan, not
improvised. If it can't be separated cleanly, phase 2 revenue drops and the carry rises
accordingly.

---

## Bottom line

**Q1: The business covers 160% of an $800k mortgage** — DSCR 1.60, comfortably financeable on
the property's own income, and payable off in under 12 years if you apply the surplus.

**Q2: 23–42 months all at once, with $250k–460k of carry and nowhere to live.
Phased, roughly 22 months to housing and month 11 to first revenue, with ~$95k of carry.**

The operating business was never the weak point. **The weak point is the 2–3 year gap between
buying and earning** — and phasing the build is what closes it.
