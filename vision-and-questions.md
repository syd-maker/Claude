# Vision & Questions — Organized by What You Care About

Six areas. Each one: **the vision**, **what you need to know**, **what to ask or look for.**
Scroll to whichever part of the conversation you're in.

# 1. 🌿 The Garden — the special thing

### The vision
A lush, layered, living garden optimized so birds and butterflies actually come. Shaded green
corridors opening into sunny, flower-packed clearings. The most peaceful corner of Marathon.

### What you need to know
| | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Where the full sun falls** | Butterflies need direct sun. This is where the garden's heart goes |
| **Prevailing wind direction** | Butterflies won't fly in wind — sets the windbreak |
| **Where water collects** | A natural low spot is a free water feature; birds bathe, butterflies puddle |
| **Which mature trees are natives** | Anything established is worth years you cannot buy |
| **What's already thriving untended** | That's your proven plant palette for this soil |
| **Soil condition in the chicken area** | Needs testing before you plant anything you'll sit in |
| **Fire ant mound locations and density** | Manageable with targeted baits — never broadcast spray |
| **Septic drainfield location** | You can't plant trees over it. Could consume real garden area |

### Ask her
- **"Where do you see the most birds and butterflies?"** ← most valuable question you'll ask
- "Which parts get brutal afternoon sun?"
- "Where does water stand after a big rain?"
- "What have you planted that thrived? What died?"
- "Is there anything out here you'd want to see kept?"

### The yard questions, in priority order

**The wildlife — ask this first**
1. **"Where do you see the most birds and butterflies?"** — wherever they already gather,
   something is working. That becomes the heart of the garden
2. "What kinds do you get? Any time of year they really show up?"
3. "Do you feed the birds, or do they just come?"

**Water — the one people forget**
4. **"Is there a well, or any irrigation out here?"** — Keys potable water is piped from the
   mainland and expensive. Irrigating a big garden on city water is a real operating cost. A
   well, even brackish and non-potable, is worth money
5. "Is there any cistern or rainwater collection?"
6. "Where does water stand after a hard rain?"

**Soil — the Keys-specific one**
7. **"How deep is the soil before you hit rock?"** — the Keys are coral caprock, often under
   very thin soil. This decides whether you plant normally, auger holes into rock, build up
   beds, or import soil by the truckload. **It's one of the biggest cost variables in the whole
   garden and nobody thinks to ask it**
8. "Has anyone ever brought in fill or topsoil?"

**Sun and wind**
9. "Which parts get brutal afternoon sun?"
10. "Where's the breeze? Any corner that's always still?"

**What grows**
11. **"What have you planted that thrived? What died?"** — free, proven data on this exact soil
12. "Any of these trees ever been trimmed hard or topped?"
13. "Has anything been removed over the years?"

**Pests and the chickens**
14. "How bad are the fire ants, and where are they worst?"
15. "What's the mosquito situation? Any season that's rough?"
16. "How long have the chickens been here, and where did the feed get stored?"

**The infrastructure that eats garden**
17. **"Sewer or septic? If septic, where's the drainfield?"** — you can't plant trees over it,
    build on it, or park on it
18. "Any buried lines, old irrigation, anything I should know is under there?"

**The relationship question**
19. **"Is there anything out here you'd want to see kept?"**

### Your line
> *"My goal is to optimize the garden so birds and butterflies come. I want to find what's
> already working here and build around it."*

# 2. 🏠 The Buildings — making it cute

### The vision
Small, charming, light-filled, elevated. Not big. The garden is the star; the houses frame it.

### ⚠️ The thing to assess carefully today
You're seeing the inside for the first time. **The real question is whether anything is
genuinely salvageable** — that's a six-figure swing either way.

But know this before you get hopeful: **the FEMA 50% rule** means if improvement costs exceed
50% of a structure's value, the whole building must come up to current flood code — including
elevation. An unlivable building blows through 50% instantly. **So "renovate" usually isn't on
the menu, even when it looks like it should be.**

Look anyway, and look for:
- Is the **structure** sound — foundation, framing, roof? Or is it cosmetic damage over a good shell?
- Any water intrusion, rot, mold, termite damage?
- Does it have real kitchens and bathrooms, or was it camping?
- **Count the electric meters.** Meter count hints at how many dwelling units the city recognizes
- Any posted red tags, condemnation notices, or stop-work orders?
- Could any **slab or foundation** be reused even if the building can't?

### What you need to know
| | Why |
|---|---|
| **FEMA base flood elevation** | Sets how high new homes must sit — drives cost |
| Whether structures were permitted **as dwellings** | Goes straight to your building rights |
| Electrical panel size | Determines what you can run later |
| Water meter size | A commercial use may need bigger |

### Ask her
- "Were these originally built as homes? Were they ever rented out?"
- "Do you have any old permits, plans, or a survey?"
- "Has anything ever flooded — and how high did the water get?"
- "What's the roof situation? When was anything last replaced?"

# 3. 🌅 How You Live There — the peaceful part

### The vision
Quiet. Private. Shaded. You wake up in it, not just next to it. Minus fire ants, minus chicken
poop.

### What you need to know
- **Where's the quietest corner?** That's where the residence goes
- What do you **hear** from each corner — road noise from 74th, neighbors, AC units?
- What do you **see** that needs screening? What's worth framing a view toward?
- Where's the breeze, and where's it still and buggy? (Mosquitoes are a real quality-of-life
  factor here)
- Where does afternoon sun hit hardest — shade matters for living, not just plants
- **Where would you naturally want to sit?** Walk it and notice. The site will tell you

### Ask her
- "Where's your favorite spot on the property?"
- "What's the mosquito situation like? Any time of year that's rough?"
- "Is it noisy? What's the street like at different times of day?"
- "What do you love most about living here?"

That last one is both the warmest question and the most informative.

# 4. ☕ Sharing It Eventually — the soft, long-term layer

### The vision
Someday people can come see the garden. Maybe something simple like coffee. Years out, not now.

### What you need to know — the ladder and its cliffs
| Rung | Triggers |
|---|---|
| Private garden, friends | Nothing |
| Open by appointment, free | Insurance |
| **Money changes hands** | Business tax receipt, zoning, parking count, ADA path |
| **Coffee cart, walk-up** | DBPR license, **commissary agreement**, food manager cert |
| **Coffee + seating** | **Restrooms**, more parking, full ADA, more septic flow |
| Permanent café | Building permit, floor area allocation |

**🔴 The single determining fact: sewer or septic.** On septic, adding food service requires
re-permitting and possibly a full system upgrade. On central sewer, it's a connection fee.

### Ask her
- **"Is it on city sewer or septic?"** ← ask early, it changes everything
- **"Has there ever been a business run on this property?"** ← a legal nonconforming use may
  attach to the land, and it's invisible unless you ask
- "Has the city ever been out here for anything?"
- "What are the neighbors like?" *(if commercial use ever needs a hearing, neighbors decide it)*

### If she asks what you'd do with it
> *"Long term we'd love people to be able to come see the garden — maybe something simple like
> coffee one day. But that's years out. Right now it's about living here and getting the garden
> going."*

# 5. 🤝 Her, and the Deal

### The vision
She hands something she loves to someone who'll love it. That's the whole transaction.

### What you need to know
- **How many building rights, and is anything in writing?** The most valuable thing on the property
- **Is there a mortgage on it?** A due-on-sale clause changes any seller-financing structure
- Her timeline, and whether there's pressure
- Has it been listed before? Did anything fall through, and why?
- What matters to her emotionally — chickens, trees, family history

### Ask her
- "Do you know how many building rights or ROGO exemptions it has?"
- "Has the city ever given you anything in writing about that?"
- **"What would you want to see happen here?"** ← then be quiet and listen
- "How long have you had it? Has it been in the family?"
- "Is there a mortgage on it currently?" *(ask casually)*
- "What's your timeline?"

### Today, don't
No price. No range. No terms. No criticism of the property. No commitments. No mention of
demolition — say "replace" or "rebuild" if it comes up at all.

# 6. 🔧 The Unglamorous Essentials

| Thing | Why it matters | How to find out |
|---|---|---|
| **Building rights** | Worth more than the dirt. Marathon has none left to issue | Ask her, then get it in writing from Planning |
| **Sewer vs septic** | Decides the coffee cart, and eats garden if septic | Ask; photograph lids and cleanouts |
| **Zoning district** | Whether commercial is allowed as-of-right or needs a hearing | City of Marathon Planning |
| **Parking count required** | Quietly caps how many visitors you could ever host | Planning |
| **FEMA flood zone + BFE** | Sets elevation, drives construction cost and insurance | Flood map / Planning |
| **Insurance quotes** | The cost most likely to blow up the plan | Get written quotes early |
| **Lot lines** | Where one lot ends and the other begins | Ask her to point; then survey |
| **Existing liens** | Affects whether seller financing is even possible | Title search |

# The one rule for today

> ## Nothing gets demolished, cleared, or "cleaned up" until the building rights are confirmed in writing.
>
> Those rights exist because the structures exist. Marathon has none left to issue. Tear one
> down first and it's gone permanently, at any price.
>
> If she kindly offers to clear the lot for you — thank her, and say you'd rather sort the
> paperwork first.

---

**You're not buying a lot. You're asking her to hand something she loves to someone who'll love
it too. Act like that and the rest gets easier.**
