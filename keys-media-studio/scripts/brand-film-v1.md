# Keys Media Studio — Brand Film Script (v1)

**Working title:** *They Buy the Feeling*
**Runtime:** ~90s hero cut · 30s and 15s cutdowns below
**Audience:** Top luxury listing agents in the Florida Keys
**Goal:** Partnership waitlist sign-ups
**Tone:** Quiet confidence. Slow, cinematic, warm. No hype music, no fast cuts until the turn.
**Voice:** One narrator, calm and low. Talks to the agent like a peer, not like an ad.

---

## Structure

1. **The truth everyone knows** (0:00–0:15)
2. **The gap** — knowing isn't the same as doing (0:15–0:30)
3. **What emotion actually is** — story (0:30–0:55)
4. **Who the luxury buyer really is** (0:55–1:10)
5. **Keys Media Studio + the agent** (1:10–1:25)
6. **The invitation** — waitlist (1:25–1:30)

---

## Script

### 1 — COLD OPEN (0:00–0:15)

**VISUAL:** Black. Sound of water against pilings. Fade up: a dock at 6:40 a.m., mist on flat water. A man's hand sets a coffee mug on the rail. We never see his face.

**SFX:** Water, a pelican, a distant outboard.

**VO:**
> Every agent knows it.
> People don't buy houses with logic.
> They buy them with emotion.

---

### 2 — THE GAP (0:15–0:30)

**VISUAL:** Hard cut to a laptop screen: a flat MLS listing. Wide-angle interior, blown-out windows. The cursor scrolls past 40 identical photos. Then a slow push-in on the empty dock from Scene 1, now shot flat and at midday, with no story.

**MUSIC:** A low piano note enters and holds.

**VO:**
> So why do most listings feel like a spreadsheet?
> Four bed. Three bath. Deep water.
> Nobody ever flew in from Manhattan for a bath count.

---

### 3 — WHAT EMOTION IS (0:30–0:55)

**VISUAL:** Back to golden hour. Sequence of intimate, human-scale moments at one property:
- Bare feet stepping onto warm coral stone.
- Tarpon rolling off the dock light at dusk.
- A screen door swinging open onto the water.
- A child's wet footprints drying on teak.
- Wide drone: the house small against endless blue, Seven Mile Bridge on the horizon.

**MUSIC:** Strings build under the piano.

**VO:**
> Emotion isn't a vibe.
> It has a structure. It's called story.
> The first coffee on the dock. The tarpon rolling at dusk.
> The moment you stop checking your phone.
> That's what they're buying.
> *(beat)*
> If you've never studied how that story gets built…
> you're leaving it to chance.

---

### 4 — THE LUXURY BUYER (0:55–1:10)

**VISUAL:** Silhouette on a flybridge at dusk. A woman at an infinity edge, back to camera, looking out. Slow motion. No faces, so the viewer steps into the frame.

**VO:**
> At this level, your buyer already has everything.
> They're not buying square footage.
> They're buying proof they made it —
> and a place that finally feels like it.
> People who've earned the best want to buy the best.
> From the best.

---

### 5 — KEYS MEDIA STUDIO (1:10–1:25)

**VISUAL:** The turn. The pace lifts. Behind the scenes: a gimbal gliding down a dock, a drone lifting off a boat bow, a director framing a shot at sunset, a colorist grading the same dock shot from flat to golden. Intercut with the agent (face finally on screen) walking a client through the finished film on an iPad. The client leans in.

**MUSIC:** Full swell.

**VO:**
> That's what we do.
> Keys Media Studio turns properties into stories —
> and agents into the name people ask for.
> Because the most desired listings
> belong to the most desired agents.

---

### 6 — INVITATION (1:25–1:30)

**VISUAL:** Back to the dock from the cold open. Same morning light. The mug is still on the rail. Slow fade to black. Logo.

**MUSIC:** Resolves to the single piano note, then silence. Water.

**VO:**
> Partnerships are open.
> By waitlist.

**SUPER:** KEYS MEDIA STUDIO
**SUPER:** Partnerships by waitlist — keysmediastudio.com *(confirm URL)*

---

## Cutdowns

**30s** — Scenes 1 → 3 (first 3 VO lines + "Emotion isn't a vibe. It's called story.") → 5 (last two lines) → 6.

**15s**
> Every agent knows buyers buy with emotion.
> Emotion is story. We build it.
> Keys Media Studio. Partnerships open — by waitlist.

---

## Production notes

- **One property, shot as one day** (dawn → dusk). It gives the film a spine and makes the grading beat in Scene 5 land.
- **Withhold faces until Scene 5.** The buyer shots stay anonymous so any viewer can picture themselves there. The agent is the first face we see, which makes the agent the hero.
- **Scene 2 must look intentionally bad.** Shoot it on a phone at midday. The contrast is the argument.
- **Casting the agent:** use a real partner agent if possible. That doubles as social proof.
- **Scarcity:** only say "by waitlist" if you actually cap partners (e.g. one per town: Key West, Marathon, Islamorada, Key Largo). If you don't, soften it to "Now accepting partners."

## Edits from the original brief

- Cut "we worked to be the best." Brand films that praise themselves read as insecure at the luxury tier. The agent is the hero, and the studio is the guide.
- Kept "people who've made it want to buy the best, from the best" almost word for word. It's the strongest line in the brief.
- Changed "most desired agent in the entire Florida Keys" to "the name people ask for." It's the same promise and sounds more natural spoken aloud.
