# Due Diligence Checklist — 74th Street, Marathon

Ordered by consequence. Items marked **STOP** are deal-breakers: if the answer comes back
wrong, you walk. Do not spend money on later items until the STOP items clear.

---

## Tier 1 — STOP items (do these first, make them closing contingencies)

- [ ] **STOP — Written BPAS/ROGO exemption determination from City of Marathon Planning.**
      How many dwelling units is this property entitled to rebuild? Get it in writing, on
      city letterhead, referencing the parcel IDs. Everything in the business plan assumes
      three. Verify three.
- [ ] **STOP — Are the existing structures still recognized as lawfully established dwelling
      units?** Ask specifically: condemned? red-tagged? abandonment lapse? permitted as
      dwellings originally, or as sheds/accessory structures? An unlivable building can still
      carry its right — or can have quietly lost it.
- [ ] **STOP — Zoning district confirmation and permitted-use letter.** Is "art instruction
      studio" permitted as-of-right or conditional? Is "food and beverage service" permitted?
      A conditional use means a public hearing, months, and no guarantee.
- [ ] **STOP — Non-residential floor area availability.** Commercial floor area is allocated
      separately from residential rights. Confirm there is allocation available for the
      studio and coffee kiosk.
- [ ] **STOP — Insurance quotes, wind and flood, on the intended new construction.** Not a
      ballpark from a friend. Written quotes. Keys insurance costs have killed more projects
      here than construction costs have.

## Tier 2 — Cost and feasibility (before removing contingencies)

- [ ] Boundary and topographic **survey**; confirm parcel count, lot lines, and whether the
      lots must be aggregated (and whether aggregation would *reduce* your rights — ask).
- [ ] Pull records from **Monroe County Property Appraiser**: parcel IDs / RE numbers,
      assessed value, ownership history, and permit history.
- [ ] **FEMA flood zone and base flood elevation** for each parcel. Determines elevation
      requirement and insurance cost.
- [ ] **FEMA 50% rule** determination on any structure you might have hoped to keep.
- [ ] **Parking requirement calculation** for the intended commercial use. This quietly caps
      your maximum class size — find the ceiling before you design around a bigger one.
- [ ] **Soil testing** across the yard, especially anywhere the chicken operation ran.
      Pathogens, heavy metals, nitrogen loading. Guests will sit in this garden and children
      may be in it.
- [ ] **Pest/infestation assessment** — identify what the infestation actually is. Chicken
      feed almost always means rodents; rodents mean remediation before food service is
      permitted anywhere on site.
- [ ] **Environmental site assessment (Phase I)** if there is any history of fuel storage,
      vehicle work, or dumping on the parcels.
- [ ] Confirm **water, sewer, and electric** service and capacity. Marathon central sewer
      connection and impact fees for added units can be a significant line item.
- [ ] **Demolition cost quotes**, including hazardous material survey (asbestos and lead are
      likely in older Keys structures).
- [ ] **Construction cost estimate** from a Keys-based contractor. Mainland numbers are not
      applicable — assume a substantial premium and a long schedule.

## Tier 3 — Business setup (after contingencies clear)

- [ ] Florida **food service license** and health inspection path for the coffee kiosk.
- [ ] **Business tax receipt** from City of Marathon and Monroe County.
- [ ] **Sales tax registration** (Florida DOR) — tickets and coffee are both taxable.
- [ ] **General liability insurance** for a public-facing experience business; confirm the
      carrier is comfortable with guests on-site, minors, and an open garden.
- [ ] Entity formation and structure — talk to a Florida CPA about separating the real estate
      from the operating business. Given you'll also live here, the personal-residence /
      commercial-use split has real tax consequences (homestead exemption, capital gains
      treatment, depreciation).
- [ ] If pursuing **Scenario B (workforce housing)**: meet Marathon Planning about affordable
      allocation availability, deed restriction terms, AMI rent caps, and tenant qualification
      requirements. Do this early — it may change the site plan.
- [ ] **Native plant sourcing** — Keys native nursery, host and nectar plant list for target
      species. Confirm nothing on the plant list is invasive or restricted.

## Tier 4 — Explicitly NOT needed (confirmed)

- [ ] ~~USDA APHIS PPQ 526 permit~~ — **not required** for an open native garden with wild,
      locally-occurring butterflies. Only triggered if you enclose the garden and import,
      move interstate, or release purchased stock. See business plan §3. If the concept ever
      shifts to an enclosed butterfly house, this becomes a Tier 1 item with a long lead time.
- [ ] ~~FDACS arthropod permit~~ — same condition as above.
