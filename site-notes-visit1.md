## 2. 🌳 You may have a state-endangered tree — do not touch it
`[?]` Your note *"Ligaments videy, purple flowers native, sought-after wood, protected"* reads
almost certainly as **Lignum vitae (Guaiacum sanctum).**

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | **Florida state-endangered** |
| Range | Miami-Dade and **Monroe County (Keys only)** |
| Flowers | Showy bluish-purple, March–August |
| Wood | Famously among the densest and most sought-after in the world |
| Notes | Withstands hurricanes easily. Some specimens on Lignumvitae Key are **over 1,000 years old** |

**Action:**
- **Do not remove, trim, or let any contractor near it.** Flag it physically before anyone works

- Its presence may affect where you can build — find out before designing
- This is a genuine asset and a genuine legal obligation at the same time

## 3. 🦎 Iguanas are a direct threat to your exact vision
You noted *"you better watch iguana."* Take this seriously, because iguanas eat **precisely the
plants a butterfly garden depends on:**

> **Passionvine** — the host plant for Gulf Fritillary and Zebra Longwing — is a **favorite
> iguana food.** They eat the leaves and the flowers.
>
> So is **firebush**, your primary nectar plant. Plus hibiscus, orchids, bougainvillea, and
> nickerbean.

Five or six adults can **defoliate a mature hibiscus hedge overnight.** The Keys are the worst
place in Florida for this — year-round warmth means continuous breeding with no cold-snap
die-off.

**The good news:** green iguanas are non-native and **not protected** in Florida. They may be
humanely removed on private property with landowner permission.

**Plan for it from day one:**
- 18-inch smooth sheet-metal **trunk collars** on palms and fruit trees
- Trim branches away from fences and rooflines — iguanas use them as highways
- Remove fallen fruit promptly
- Favor plants they avoid where possible: citrus, oleander, milkweed, pigeon plum, some crotons
- Ongoing trapping/removal — this is maintenance, not a one-time fix

**This is a real operating cost and it was invisible until you walked the property.**

## 4. 🏪 They ran a plant business here — was it permitted?
You noted they *"were selling"* waterlilies `[?]` and you asked whether the greenhouse needed

If a commercial nursery use was ever legally established, there may be a **legal nonconforming

**Ask:** "Was the plant-selling ever licensed or permitted? Did the city ever approve the

# 🌍 SOIL — question answered

> **"Most of this property is all coral"** — and **they added a lot of dirt over time for the
> gardens.**

So the growing medium is **man-made, built up over 50 years on top of caprock.** Two
consequences:

**The good:** your established beds have real soil that took decades to build. That's an asset
you could not buy.

**The caution:** soil depth **varies and is shallow where they didn't build it up.** Any new
planting area needs the same treatment — imported soil, raised beds, or augering into rock.
**Ask them how deep the coral is in different spots** and map it.
